Attention-Based DNN for Semantic-Importance-Aware Scheduling in 6G-Oriented IoT Networks

Real IoT Data – Attention-Based Deep Learning – Wireless Network Evaluation

In a busy IoT network, should a DDoS attack alert wait behind a smart fridge's temperature update?

Most networks serve traffic first-come-first-served. Our attention-based DNN reads each IoT traffic record, decides how important it is, and lets the wireless scheduler send urgent traffic first.

Trained on 211,043 real TON_IoT records and evaluated with wireless metrics: latency, deadline misses, goodput, spectral efficiency, BER and PER.

What this means

Throughput and spectral efficiency are almost identical for all schedulers: the channel carries the same bits, AB-DNN only changes which bits go first. The gain in latency and on-time data costs no extra spectrum.

06

Method & dataset

Dataset and labels

TON_IoT network dataset (UNSW Canberra), train_test_network.csv, 211,043 records. 60,000 stratified de-duplicated records were used (42,000 / 9,000 / 9,000). Importance comes from the dataset's own type label:

LOW normal   MEDIUM scanning, password, xss, mitm   HIGH dos, ddos, injection, backdoor, ransomware

Moustafa N., Sustainable Cities and Society (2021) 102994; Alsaedi A. et al., IEEE Access 8 (2020) 165130–165150.

Models and wireless model

Baseline DNN: Dense 128 → Dropout 0.3 → Dense 64 → Softmax.
AB-DNN: attention block α = softmax(W₂ tanh(W₁x)) re-weights the 106 input features, then the same DNN.

Uplink: 20 MHz, 1 ms slots, QPSK (40,000 bits/slot), Rayleigh fading, BER = ½·erfc(√γ), PER = 1 − (1 − BER)L, up to 4 HARQ attempts. Latency budgets: HIGH 10 ms, MEDIUM 50 ms, LOW 200 ms.

The IoT records are real; the channel and queue are simulated with standard textbook models.
